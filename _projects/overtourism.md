We show here an approach to the quantification of overtourism from origin-destination datasets.
We here show a proof of concept with flows simulated by gravity model with not-fitted parameters (randomly initialized) and from freely available bus data [Link Text](https://www.trentinotrasporti.it/open-data).
The analysis is thought to be applied for given (`day`, `time interval`).    
We differentiate `in` fluxes with `out` fluxes. 
The goal of the analysis is to define the set of fluxes that are critical.   
The main idea for this project is in `Hierarchical organization of urban mobility and its connection with city livability` of `Bassolas et al.`. 
The idea is pretty simple and is a non-parametric way to quantify the most critical flows giving a criterion to avoid considering all the informations $N_e = \frac{N_{polygons}*(N_{polygons}-1)}{2}$.
The in/out fluxes are ordered by their intensity. They form a Lorenz curve (ordered cumulative pdf) L: [0,1] -> [0,1]. 
We apply iteratively the Loubar criterion to extract the most relevant fluxes. The Loubar criterion is simply computing the derivative of the Lorenz curve at x = 1 and intersect on the x-axis in x*. All the $x \ge x*$ are considered to be hotspots. By iteratively applying the method and extracting the hotspot we obtain a hierarchy of hotspots considering both `in` and `out` flows. The information is therefore defining hotspot as critical places according to the total `in` or `out` flows and now we want to understand what are the flows that drive these criticality.     
So for each place in a given hotspot level we run the Hotspot hierarcical analysis on the fluxes.
In the case of `in` flows we consider all the flows that have as destination the place on the hotspot level.
Applying the Loubar principle on the Lorenz curve on flows so chosen then we define the critical flows.
